02 / UNDER THE SURFACE

NOT ALL BODY FAT BEHAVES THE SAME.

Visceral fat is fat stored deeper inside the abdominal cavity, surrounding organs. Unlike the fat stored just beneath the skin, it is metabolically active and can influence processes involved in inflammation and metabolism.

That is why the conversation around visceral fat is really a conversation about metabolic health — not simply about how someone looks.

SCIENCE NOTE

Research links higher visceral fat levels with cardiometabolic risks, including insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

03 / WHY IT MATTERS

THE HIDDEN CONNECTION.

Higher visceral fat is associated with several cardiometabolic risk factors. It is one piece of a much bigger health picture.

01

Blood Glucose

Visceral fat is associated with insulin resistance and increased type 2 diabetes risk.

02

Heart Health

Higher visceral fat is associated with cardiovascular risk.

03

Inflammation

Excess visceral fat can be linked with inflammatory processes in the body.

04

Metabolism

Visceral fat is metabolically active and interacts with systems that regulate energy and glucose.

04 / MYTH CHECK

LET'S CLEAR THE NOISE.

Visceral fat is often surrounded by oversimplified fitness claims. Here are a few important distinctions.

01 / 03
MYTH

You can target visceral fat with one specific exercise.

FACT

There is no single exercise that selectively removes visceral fat. Overall physical activity and healthy lifestyle habits matter more than one "magic" movement.

05 / KNOW YOUR SIGNALS

YOUR BODY LEAVES CLUES.

You cannot reliably tell how much visceral fat someone has simply by looking at them. Health professionals use measurements and clinical information to assess risk.

Waist circumference can be one useful risk marker, but it does not diagnose visceral fat or diabetes by itself.

01
Waist measurement

Can help estimate abdominal-fat related health risk.

02
Blood glucose

Blood tests can help identify prediabetes or diabetes.

03
Blood pressure

A routine health measurement relevant to cardiometabolic risk.

04
Medical assessment

A healthcare professional can interpret your measurements together.

06 / THE BIGGER PICTURE

DON'T CHASE A NUMBER.

Long-term metabolic health is built from consistent habits — not extreme shortcuts.

01

Move Regularly

Regular physical activity supports overall metabolic and cardiovascular health.

02

Eat Consistently Well

Build meals around a balanced variety of nutritious foods rather than extreme diets.

03

Protect Your Sleep

Healthy sleep is an important part of overall wellbeing and daily recovery.

04

Think Health First

Focus on energy, strength, fitness and wellbeing rather than appearance alone.
